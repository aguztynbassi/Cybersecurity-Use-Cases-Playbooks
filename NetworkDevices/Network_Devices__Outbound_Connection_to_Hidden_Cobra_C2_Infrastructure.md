# Outbound Connection to Hidden Cobra C2 Infrastructure

## Description
Alert generated by the `Outbound Connection to Hidden Cobra C2 Infrastructure` detection. Detects outbound network connections from internal hosts to IP addresses identified as belonging to Hidden Cobra (also known as Lazarus Group, APT38), a sophisticated state-sponsored threat actor. These connections are strong indicators of active Command and Control (C2) communication or data exfiltration, signaling a critical compromise of the connecting system. This rule requires an up-to-date threat intelligence feed for `HIDDEN_COBRA_IPS`. The alert must be reviewed by the SOC to validate its legitimacy, scope, affected asset, and potential impact.

## Objective
Detect and prioritize potential malicious, anomalous, or unauthorized activity related to network device (firewall, router, switch, or IDS/IPS sensor), enabling the SOC to initiate a structured investigation, reduce operational risk, and activate containment procedures when indicators of compromise are identified.

## Logic
The detection is triggered by the `Outbound Connection to Hidden Cobra C2 Infrastructure` rule. The prioritization logic should consider severity, asset criticality, event recurrence, network source/destination, involved identity, platform, and correlated activity in supporting data sources.

Patterns or conditions to validate:

- Exact match of the detection name/signature with this use case.
- Severity equal to or above the SOC-defined threshold.
- Production, critical, publicly exposed, or sensitive-data asset.
- IP address, domain, user, process, or resource not recognized by the asset/account owner.
- Logs or telemetry from the supporting data sources occurring close to the first or last observed event.
- Sensitive changes following the event, such as permission changes, new persistence, credential use, logging changes, public exposure, command execution, or data access.

## Investigation

Mandatory 5W Questions:

- **Who:** Which user, host, service account, process, or external actor is involved?
- **What:** What action was observed, and what asset was affected? Was there access, execution, a configuration change, network communication, or an authentication attempt?
- **When:** When did the first and last observations occur? Did the activity take place outside expected business hours or near another change?
- **Where:** From which host, IP address, country, ASN, or platform did the activity originate?
- **Why:** Is there an operational justification, approved automation, authorized testing activity, or planned change that explains the event?

## Evidence for Determination

### False Positives

- The `Outbound Connection to Hidden Cobra C2 Infrastructure` rule has not yet been tuned to this environment and is matching a legitimate, documented pattern - confirm with the asset/account owner before escalating.
- An approved backup, replication, or data-sharing job legitimately transfers data at a similar volume or destination.
- A security tool, EDR agent, or sandbox deliberately connected to a known-bad indicator as part of signature testing or detonation analysis.
- The destination was recently reassigned by a hosting provider and no longer hosts malicious infrastructure.

### True Positives

- This rule requires an up-to-date threat intelligence feed for `HIDDEN_COBRA_IPS`.
- The transfer targets a destination or volume with no legitimate business relationship to the source, and follows a collection or credential-theft indicator.
- The transfer uses a channel (DNS, email, an unusual protocol) chosen specifically to blend in or evade monitored egress paths.
- The host or account is communicating at a regular interval with infrastructure on an active threat-intelligence list, with no legitimate business reason to do so.
- The communication coincides with an unexplained process, scheduled task, or new credential usage on the same host.
- Multiple internal hosts begin communicating with the same external destination in a short window.

### True Negatives

- The transfer is confirmed to be an approved, documented data movement.
- The destination is an approved, documented integration or vendor endpoint.

### False Negatives

- Data is exfiltrated slowly, in small volumes, over a long period to stay under volumetric thresholds.
- The channel used is not currently monitored by this detection's data sources.
- The C2 channel is routed through a reputable CDN or cloud provider (domain fronting), blending in with legitimate traffic.
- The beacon interval is randomized and infrequent enough to stay below this detection's threshold.
