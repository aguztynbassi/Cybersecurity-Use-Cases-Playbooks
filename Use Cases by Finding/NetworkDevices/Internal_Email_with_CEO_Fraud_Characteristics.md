# Internal Email with CEO Fraud Characteristics (Spoofed Sender)

## Description
Alert generated by the `Internal Email with CEO Fraud Characteristics (Spoofed Sender)` detection. Detects fraudulent emails with 'CEO Fraud' or internal spoofing characteristics. An email that appears to originate from a key executive in the organization is received by an internal user, but its origin ('OriginalClientAddress') does not correspond to a legitimate mail server, suggesting a compromised internal account or a phishing attempt. The alert must be reviewed by the SOC to validate its legitimacy, scope, affected asset, and potential impact.

## Objective
Detect and prioritize potential malicious, anomalous, or unauthorized activity related to network device (firewall, router, switch, or IDS/IPS sensor), enabling the SOC to initiate a structured investigation, reduce operational risk, and activate containment procedures when indicators of compromise are identified.

## Logic
The detection is triggered by the `Internal Email with CEO Fraud Characteristics (Spoofed Sender)` rule. The prioritization logic should consider severity, asset criticality, event recurrence, network source/destination, involved identity, platform, and correlated activity in supporting data sources.

Patterns or conditions to validate:

- Exact match of the detection name/signature with this use case.
- Severity equal to or above the SOC-defined threshold.
- Production, critical, publicly exposed, or sensitive-data asset.
- IP address, domain, user, process, or resource not recognized by the asset/account owner.
- Logs or telemetry from the supporting data sources occurring close to the first or last observed event.
- Sensitive changes following the event, such as permission changes, new persistence, credential use, logging changes, public exposure, command execution, or data access.

## Evidence for Determination

### False Positives

- The `Internal Email with CEO Fraud Characteristics (Spoofed Sender)` rule has not yet been tuned to this environment and is matching a legitimate, documented pattern - confirm with the asset/account owner before escalating.
- An approved administrator or automation legitimately required and obtained elevated privileges through a documented process.
- A security-testing tool intentionally exercised the privilege-escalation path as part of an approved assessment.
- An approved penetration test or security assessment performed the activity from a documented source during a scheduled engagement window.
- A legitimate but newly onboarded integration, partner, or user is exercising the access path for the first time.

### True Positives

- An email that appears to originate from a key executive in the organization is received by an internal user, but its origin ('OriginalClientAddress') does not correspond to a legitimate mail server, suggesting a compromised internal account or a phishing attempt.
- An account or process gains privileges it does not normally hold, with no corresponding approval or ticket.
- The elevation is followed by actions normally reserved for administrators.
- The technique used matches a known, documented privilege-escalation exploit or misconfiguration abuse.
- The access attempt succeeds and is followed by further activity inconsistent with the account's or application's normal behavior.
- The source has no plausible legitimate relationship to the targeted account, application, or service.

### True Negatives

- The elevation matches an approved administrative workflow.
- The activity is confirmed as an approved test or a legitimate first-time integration.

### False Negatives

- The attacker already holds sufficient privileges from an earlier, undetected compromise, so no escalation event occurs for this detection to catch.
- Escalation is achieved through a legitimate-looking, pre-existing misconfiguration rather than an exploit this detection watches for.
- The initial access vector evades signature-based detection (e.g. a novel exploit, a credential obtained outside any monitored channel).
- The attacker succeeds on the very first attempt, leaving no earlier failed attempts to correlate against.
