# SolarisLDAP Group Remove from LDAP Detected

## Description
Alert generated by the `SolarisLDAP Group Remove from LDAP Detected` detection. Detects the removal of a group from Solaris LDAP. Adversaries may remove groups to disrupt operations, remove evidence of their activities, or to disable security controls. This is a strong indicator of an adversary attempting to impact the environment or evade security defenses. The alert must be reviewed by the SOC to validate its legitimacy, scope, affected asset, and potential impact.

## Objective
Detect and prioritize potential malicious, anomalous, or unauthorized activity related to Windows host, enabling the SOC to initiate a structured investigation, reduce operational risk, and activate containment procedures when indicators of compromise are identified.

- Sensitive changes following the event, such as permission changes, new persistence, credential use, logging changes, public exposure, command execution, or data access.

## Evidence for Determination

### False Positives

- The `SolarisLDAP Group Remove from LDAP Detected` rule has not yet been tuned to this environment and is matching a legitimate, documented pattern - confirm with the asset/account owner before escalating.
- An approved maintenance window, decommissioning process, or disaster-recovery test legitimately performed the destructive or disruptive action.
- A legitimate software installer, IT management tool, or approved automation created the persistence mechanism as part of its normal function.
- An administrator intentionally configured the mechanism (service, scheduled task, startup entry) for a documented operational reason.

### True Positives

- This is a strong indicator of an adversary attempting to impact the environment or evade security defenses.
- The action (deletion, encryption, service disruption, configuration change) has no corresponding change ticket or administrator acknowledgment.
- It follows other compromise indicators on the same host or account and targets production or business-critical assets.
- The persistence mechanism points to an unfamiliar binary, script, or location with no legitimate business purpose.
- The mechanism was created shortly after another suspicious event on the same host (new login, exploitation, suspicious download).
- No change ticket or administrator acknowledges the change when the SOC follows up.

### True Negatives

- The action is confirmed to be part of an approved, scheduled operation.
- The change is tied to an approved deployment or administrative action.

### False Negatives

- The destructive action is narrow and quiet enough (a single file, a brief outage) to stay under this detection's threshold.
- The persistence mechanism is deliberately disguised to resemble a legitimate, commonly-seen entry.
- Persistence is established through a technique this detection does not specifically cover.
